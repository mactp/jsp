About Us

About Us

The Friends of Jacob Smith Park are passionate about enhancing and protecting this precious green space for future generations

Who are the Friends of Jacob Smith Park?

The Friends of Jacob Smith Park (FoJSP) are a Community Interest Group which was formed in 2008, shortly after JSP opened to the public.

We are passionate about enhancing the habitat to increase the biodiversity in the park and also to make improvements to help make your visit a more enjoyable one. See our recent projects (LINK TO SECTION LATER ON THIS PAGE)

FoJSP is run by a committee who provide the link between the community and North Yorkshire Council (NYC).

The committee are all volunteers and are generously supported by a wider network of enthusiastic volunteers from the local community who support work and projects in the park.

FoJSP work closely with NYC, which now owns the park and is responsible for its upkeep, e.g. cutting grass, maintaining the perimeter wall and managing the trees.

What does FoJSP do?

  • Represent the interests of the local community and park users in relation to the park
  • Agree priorities with NYC and help to deliver the yearly plan for the park
  • Fundraising for agreed improvements to the park outside the council’s responsibility
  • Arrange volunteering events to carry out agreed enhancements
  • Hold public meetings twice a year to share progress and gather views on upcoming priorities – see links:

Recent projects

  • Benches - raised funds and managed the purchase and installation of seven new oak benches to provide spots to rest during a walk or just enjoy the park environment
  • Bat & bird boxes - installed a variety of bat and bird boxes to encourage a range of species to nest in the park, including owls, robins, nuthatches, bats and many more
  • Himalayan balsam control - succeeded over three years to reduce Himalayan balsam to a manageable level. Himalayan balsam is an invasive plant species that was spreading through the park and crowding out native plants
  • Information board - installed an information board near the park entrance. The board explains the history of the park and provides a brief summary of the habitats and wildlife it contains
  • Trees - planted over 300 trees to create extra woodland habitat
  • Wildflowers - cleared dozens of areas around the park and planted hundreds of native wildflowers to increase biodiversity and support a range of insects and other wildlife
  • Shrubs – planted over 200 shrubs to provide cover for wildlife, nectar when they flower, leaves to feed insect larvae, fruit for birds and good autumn colour for the overall look of the park
  • Talks – arranged a variety of talks from experts on topics relevant to the park including:
    • Wildflower meadows and their management
    • Honey bees, bee keeping and their role in pollination
    • The physical and psychological benefits of being in parks & wild spaces
    • Bumble bees and their role in pollination
    • Dog trust – tips on owning and training dogs and how to prepare for bonfire night
    • Astronomy – an overview of what can be seen in the night sky and a closer look at selected stars and planets
  • Ponds – worked with North Yorkshire Council who commissioned Yorkshire Wildlife Trust to renovate two wildlife ponds which had dried out, aiming to provide habitat for great crested newts and other wildlife
  • Fences – supported North Yorkshire Council who managed the replacement of broken fences around veteran trees, to protect the trees’ root systems and help preserve them
